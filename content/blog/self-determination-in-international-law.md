## A Right, Not a Slogan

The right to self-determination is enshrined in Article 1 of both the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights and the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights: "All peoples have the right to freely determine their political status." It is not a fringe demand — it is one of the foundational principles of the post-war international order.

## Why It Applies Here

International law distinguishes between "internal" self-determination — autonomy and political participation within an existing state — and "external" self-determination, which can include separate statehood, typically reserved for cases of colonial domination, foreign occupation, or where a people's fundamental rights are persistently and seriously violated by the state they live under.

IDCTE's position is that decades of structural discrimination, the 2009 mass atrocities, and the continued absence of any credible path to accountability place the Tamil nation's claim within this second category. We argue that repeated failed attempts at internal power-sharing arrangements — and their consistent unraveling — are themselves evidence that internal self-determination has not been, and will not be, delivered in good faith.

## What This Means for Our Advocacy

This is why IDCTE frames its political ask carefully: not as a request for minority protections within the existing Sri Lankan state, but as a call to recognize the Tamil nation's right to determine its own political future. It's a distinction that shapes every meeting we have with foreign ministries and international institutions.
